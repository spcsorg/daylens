# Landing (Persuade)

Scope: the V2 public landing at /, with privacy, devlog and press inheriting its world.
Audience: people whose work is spread across many apps, mostly on Windows, including non-technical owners. Job: understand Daylens in five seconds, believe it is safe, join the beta waitlist.
Action: the email waitlist form, working, in the first viewport and again at the close.
Proof: an example day hung as tickets, searchable on the page. Labelled as an example. No testimonials, counts or screenshots exist.
Decided without the user (asleep, told not to be asked): the assigned direction was built without a decision round; code-led because no image generation is available.

## Direction contract

THESIS: Your day hangs in order like tickets on a kitchen order rail, each hour written in plain words. Refuses the centred headline over a product screenshot and three feature cards.

OWN-WORLD: Brushed steel rail on a cool counter-white ground; guest-check paper in pale green with ruled lines; check numbers and the "now" mark in red ink; blue-black ballpoint ink for text. One condensed-to-normal grotesk (Archivo, width axis) does headings, ticket print and body.

STORY: The visitor reads the promise, sees their kind of day already written up, searches it in their own half-remembered words, learns it never leaves their computer, and joins the beta.

FIRST VIEWPORT: Left, the headline at display scale with one line under it and the email form with a Windows/Mac choice. Across the full width beneath, the steel rail carrying five hour tickets, the last one live. The search field sits on the rail.

FORM: Kitchen order rail, position 3 of 7 on my ordered list, seed ce22a3c4.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance

Kept from declined challengers: the drum machine's chase light (the rail always shows where now is); the catalog's strict numbering (every ticket carries a check number in sequence); the alphabet storm's type-as-object (the headline is the hero's largest object); the daylight section's tabular time stamps.
